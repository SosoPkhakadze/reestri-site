From the data · story 3

We went looking for Armenia's December rush. The money is signed in January.

Every procurement system is supposed to have one: budget money spent in a hurry before 31 December, awarded without competition, pushed through urgent procedures. We tested it against 235,570 signed contracts in PPCM, Armenia's public contracts registry. December is the busiest month by contract count and an ordinary month by money. The peak is at the other end of the calendar, and it is not a rush at all.

By Soso Pkhakadze · published 9 Sep 2026 · registry index of 6 Sep 2026 · aggregate only, state buyers and companies only

The headline

The procurement year is front loaded, not back loaded

December is the busiest month and an unremarkable one for money. It holds 20,094 contracts, 10.45 percent of the count against 8.33 percent for an even month, but its 424,043,419,447 AMD is only 10.71 percent of the value. January holds 638,976,946,239 AMD, 16.14 percent, 1.94 times an even month and 1.51 times December's money. January and February together take 29.02 percent of the value on 19.72 percent of the contracts. The quiet end of the year is autumn: October holds 5.22 percent of the value, November 5.83.

The December bump rests on two years. Its share of the year's value is below an even month in four of the eight complete years: 3.46 percent in 2018, 6.74 in 2022, 7.42 in 2023, 6.10 in 2025. It clears an even month in 2019 (9.60), 2024 (9.90) and then twice by a lot, in 2020 (29.03) and 2021 (19.58). Take those two years out and there is no pattern left to explain.

No buyer of any size runs a December cliff. Among the 129 state buyers that signed at least 50 contracts worth at least 500,000,000 AMD between 2018 and 2025, the median share of value signed in December is 7.69 percent, below what an even month would give them. One is above half, three above a third, eight above a quarter. The most concentrated buyer in the whole registry is the Migration Service of the Territorial Administration Ministry at 83.07 percent, on a lifetime total of 1,548,737,498 AMD.

December contracts are not bigger and are not more urgent. The median December contract is 450,000 AMD against 434,850 for the rest of the year; the mean is 21,150,353 against 20,609,701. Urgent procedure forms carry 29.79 percent of December value against 29.43 percent for the other eleven months. Pooled by month, December ranks ninth of twelve on the urgent share of its own value; eight months run higher, from February at 43.68 percent down to July at 32.54. December is more single source (36.77 percent of contracts against 32.65), and by value that gap comes almost entirely from December 2020 and December 2021; in 2023 and 2024 December was less single source than the rest of its year.

What January really is: the state medical order. In January 2018 the Ministry of Health signed 73.19 percent of the month's value and 89.24 percent of the month was awarded single source. January 2019: 71.92 and 87.82. January 2020: 68.18 and 83.55. January 2023: 56.15 and 59.20. Pooled over the eight years, 61.25 percent of January value is single source, the highest of any month, and 3.37 percent is urgent, the lowest of any month. PPCM records state-order payments to public hospitals as single-source contracts, and they are signed at the top of the budget year. That is a booking convention, not a spending sprint.

Since 2024 the peak moved to February, and changed character. February 2024 held 14.69 percent of that year's value with 51.04 percent of the month awarded under urgent procedures and the City Planning Committee taking 46.35 percent of it. February 2025 held 27.79 percent of the year with 70.72 percent urgent and the Armenian Territorial Development Fund taking 66.08 percent. The front of the Armenian procurement year is now large accelerated construction tenders rather than annual medical contracts.

Method and caveats

What was counted, and what the data cannot say

  • Signed, not spentPPCM publishes the date a contract was signed, never the date money moved. An annual or framework contract signed in January and drawn down all year loads January. This is the main reason to read the January peak as a signing calendar rather than a spending one, and it cuts the same way against reading December as one.
  • What counts as urgentPPCM's own two procedure forms, ՀՄԱ urgent single source and ՀԲՄ urgent open tender. They are the only labels in the registry whose English name begins with "urgent". The run writes out the whole procedure vocabulary and a check field that fills if a new urgent form appears. Note the overlap: urgent single source is counted both as urgent and as single source, and the two families are never added together.
  • Complete years onlyEvery December comparison uses 2018 to 2025, 192,369 contracts worth 3,958,215,584,070 AMD. The snapshot stops on 6 September 2026, so 2026 has no December and is shown as year to date or left out. February 2026 looks enormous against a partial year and is used in no finding.
  • ExclusionsSix records carry keyed-in totals up to 1.66 quadrillion AMD while their current value is under 1.5 billion; records above 50 billion AMD are dropped from value sums and kept in counts, and all six are listed with dates in summary3.json. 3 contracts have no usable date. 21,456 are dated outside 2018 to 2026, of which 21,390 fall in 2000 to 2017 where PPCM coverage is thin and 66 carry impossible years. 867 contracts carry no value and pull medians down slightly.
  • Procedure form, not legal groundPPCM records which procedure was used, never why. An urgent award can be a real emergency, a government instruction, or a schedule that slipped. Nothing here is a finding about legality, and the Ministry of Health's January volume is state-order payments to public hospitals, an accounting convention as much as a procurement choice.
  • Two unusual DecembersDecember 2020 held 29.03 percent of that year's value, with the Health Ministry at 64.03 percent of the month and 91.28 percent of the month's money signed from 22 December. December 2021 held 19.58 percent. Read those as events, not as a recurring pattern. Nothing here was reviewed by a lawyer or by PPCM; rerun the script against the next weekly index and the figures update.
  • PrivacyAggregate only. Buyers are state and community bodies. Supplier names and tax IDs appear only for legal entities; individual entrepreneurs are counted in totals but never listed.

Story 1: single-source awards, 2018 to 2026 →

Single-source awards fell from 52.04 to 6.83 percent of contract value while staying near four in ten procedures by count. The Ministry of Health signed 486.7 billion AMD without competition, and 1,080 companies get over 90 percent of their public revenue from one buyer.

Story 2: where the single-source money went →

The Health Ministry's share of its own spend fell from 95.70 to 5.98 percent. The money moved to the Public Broadcaster, Yerevan Municipality, the High-Tech Industry Ministry and the Probation Service, while the supplier base grew 2.5 times.
