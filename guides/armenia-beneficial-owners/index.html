How to find the beneficial owners of an Armenian company

Armenia is the one country in the region that publishes who owns a company for free: the declarations sit on the company card at e-register.moj.am, in English, with a JSON download. This guide covers the three numbers a company carries, where the declarations are, what self-declared data cannot tell you, and where to read the same company's public contracts.

Identifiers

Tax ID, registration number, unique identifier: three numbers, one company

An Armenian company carries three identifiers and people mix them up constantly. The tax identification number (ՀՎՀՀ, HVHH, 8 digits, for example 02560453) is the one on invoices, contracts, bank forms and the procurement registry; if a document gives you one number, it is this one. The registration number (format 211.190.00190) is the state register's own serial and appears on the registration certificate. The unique identifier (8 digits, for example 39014171) is the key in the register website's URLs and nowhere else.

The register's search box takes a name, not a number. So the working method is: search the name, open the card, and confirm the tax ID on the card against the tax ID on your document. Names repeat (there are many "Ararat" companies); tax IDs do not.

The free layer

The company card at e-register.moj.am, in English

The State Register of Legal Entities is run by the Ministry of Justice. The 2025 site replaced the old VXSoft application (still online at old.e-register.am for older records) and has a full English interface, including English legal names where the company registered one.

  1. Open e-register.moj.am/en/search/companies. The path prefix sets the language: /en/, /hy/, /ru/.
  2. Type part of the name. The search is a substring match on the registered name, in whichever script it was registered; try the Latin name first, then the Armenian one from the company's own site. Results stop at 200, so narrow a common word.
  3. Open the card. The URL ends in the unique identifier. Read the status sentence first, then compare the tax ID with your document.
  4. Scroll to "Company BOR declarations". Each dated link is one beneficial-ownership declaration. No links means the company has declared nothing, or is exempt; see the limits section before you conclude anything.

A real card, read on 5 Sep 2026: Ameriabank, a public company. Two things to notice. The status is a sentence, not a word; that sentence means "active", and a company in liquidation gets a different sentence naming the process. And the address is Armenian even on the English page. What the free card does not show: founders, the director, the charter, activity codes. Those are in the paid certified extract from the register (AMD 8,000, one to three days), which is how the declarations become the practical way to see the people behind a company.

The owners

Reading a beneficial-ownership declaration

Armenia adopted the Beneficial Ownership Data Standard (BODS) with Open Ownership's help. Mining companies declared first, from 2021; the obligation has since been extended sector by sector. Each declaration page renders four sections (A to D: the company, the owners, the nature of control, the indirect chain) and carries a download button that hands you the whole declaration as a BODS 0.2 JSON array, the same format used by the UK and other publishers.

What a declaration names: each natural person behind the company in Armenian script and Latin transliteration, their nationality, the kind of interest (shareholding, voting rights, right to appoint the board, other influence or control), whether it is direct or indirect, the date it started, a politically-exposed-person flag, and the legal ground on which the person counts as an owner. Ground 3 means ownership is dispersed and the person named is the general manager, which is a director, not an owner; read the ground before you write "owned by".

What it does not carry: the share percentage. The structured data records the interest type but not the size, and the HTML view rarely states it either. For the exact split you still need the certified extract.

A company with several declarations has a time series. Kapan Mining and Processing Company, a mining CJSC, has 18 dated declarations on its card; the newest is the current state and the older ones show when owners changed. Take the newest for "who owns it now" and keep the list for "since when".

The honest map

What the declarations cannot tell you

  • Self-declaredThe company files the declaration about itself. Nobody at the register verifies the chain. A declaration is evidence of what the company chose to state on that date, and Open Ownership's own reviews of Armenian data note quality gaps.
  • Partial coverageMany companies have filed nothing. In our own sample of mining and media companies, an active mining CJSC in an obligated sector had an empty row. A missing declaration is not evidence of a hidden owner; it is evidence of a missing declaration.
  • Listed companies exemptCompanies whose shares trade on a regulated market do not declare (the bank above). Their ownership is in their stock-exchange disclosures instead.
  • Possibly staleA 2021 declaration with nothing after it may be current or may be three owners ago. The card date is the only clue; the extract is the check.
  • Home addressesSome declarations include the owner's residential address. It is on a public page, and it is still personal data under Armenia's data-protection law. Do not republish it; the tools below never return it.
  • Older records live elsewhereDeclarations filed before the 2025 platform sit on old.e-register.am under a different company ID. If the new card looks thin for a company you know declared earlier, look there.

Follow the contracts

Follow the tax ID into the procurement registry: public contracts on armeps.am

Armenia's public procurement registry (PPCM, part of ARMEPS at armeps.am/ppcm/public/contracts) lists every signed state contract with the buying authority, the supplier, the value, the signing date, the procedure and any appeal. Every row carries the supplier's 8-digit tax ID, the same ՀՎՀՀ you confirmed on the company card, so the two sources join without name matching.

Search by supplier and you get a company's whole public-sector income at a glance: who buys from it, how much, and how often the award was single-source (ՄԱ, a contract signed without competition). That last column is where most Armenian procurement stories start. From the 236,159 contracts in our weekly index (registry as of 29 Aug 2026): single-source awards fell from 52.04 percent of contract value in 2018 to 6.83 percent in 2026, while by count roughly four procedures in ten are still awarded without competition; 1,080 companies get over 90 percent of their public revenue from a single state buyer, and 618 have exactly one buyer. The full numbers, the CSVs and the caveats are in two open analyses: single-source awards, 2018 to 2026 and where the single-source money moved.

Two practical notes. The registry shows supplier names in the script they registered in, so an English name search can miss a company that registered in Armenian; the tax ID does not miss. And the Ministry of Finance has contracted a new procurement platform with a launch target of 1 Jan 2027, so bookmark the tax ID, not the URL.
