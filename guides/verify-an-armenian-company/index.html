Is this Armenian company real? Verify it by tax ID

Someone sent you an invoice, a contract, a "certificate of registration" or an investment offer from a company in Armenia. Start here

The tax ID: 8 digits and nothing else

Every Armenian company has a taxpayer identification number, ՀՎՀՀ (HVHH, sometimes written TIN or VAT number on English invoices). The State Revenue Committee describes it plainly: it "consists of 8 digits; no other symbols could be used", the first seven are a serial number and the eighth is a check digit, and the numbers carry no meaning. It is issued at registration by the State Register (for commercial banks, by the Central Bank), it never changes, and it is not reissued after a company is liquidated (SRC's TIN sheet at the OECD, read 13 Sep 2026).

So the first test costs nothing: count the digits. Ameriabank's tax ID is 02560453; Ucom's is 00024873. Leading zeros are part of the number. Anything with letters, dashes, nine digits or a country prefix is not an Armenian tax ID as printed by the register, however official the document looks. The SRC does not publish how the check digit is calculated, so do not trust a "validator" website; the register is the validator.

Do not confuse it with the two other numbers on an Armenian company card: the registration number (format 211.190.00190, the state register's serial) and the unique identifier (8 digits, used in the register website's addresses). Our beneficial-owners guide explains all three.

Step one

Search the state register by tax ID

The State Register of Legal Entities, run by the Ministry of Justice, is free, in English, and needs no account for searching. As of 13 Sep 2026 its search box resolves an exact tax ID and a registration number, not only a name.

  1. Open e-register.moj.am/en/search/companies. The /en/ in the address sets English; /hy/ and /ru/ exist too.
  2. Paste the 8-digit tax ID, leading zeros included. An exact tax ID returns one company: 02560453 returns AMERIABANK. A fragment returns nothing (seven digits of the same number gave 0 results when we tried), so a typo looks exactly like a company that does not exist. Check the digits before you conclude anything.
  3. If you only have a name, search the name, then open each candidate and compare its tax ID with your document. Names repeat; tax IDs do not.
  4. Open the card and read the status sentence first. An active company reads "There is no information recorded in the unified state register regarding being in the process of liquidation or the termination of activity (existence)." A company being wound up gets a different sentence naming the process. Some cards add a line such as "Attention: Restriction is applied."; the free card does not say what kind, so ask the company and consider ordering the extract.
  5. Compare every field with your paper: name, registration number, registration date, tax ID, address. The next section is the checklist.

A card proves the company is registered and what it filed, not that the people behind it are honest.

Step two

Hold the certificate against the card

A forged registration certificate rarely survives a field-by-field comparison with the official record: forgers either borrow a real company and change details, or invent a company and a number. Each line below is a mismatch we would stop at.

  • Tax IDExactly 8 digits, and the same 8 digits on the card. A tax ID that returns another company means the document borrowed a real number.
  • Registration numberIn the register's dotted format (three groups, like 211.190.00190) and identical to the card, dots included.
  • Name and legal formThe card shows the registered name and form (LLC, CJSC, OJSC and so on). A brand, a "Group" or "Holding" that the register does not know is not the legal entity.
  • Registration dateA certificate claiming 2012 for a company the card shows registered last year is a forgery or a different entity. A later date on the card can also follow a reorganisation, so ask before you accuse.
  • StatusA certificate of good standing dated this month for a company whose card says it is in liquidation is worthless.
  • IssuerArmenian companies are registered by the State Register of Legal Entities Agency of the Ministry of Justice. Applications can be handed in at some banks and Haypost branches, but the registration is the Agency's (FAQ). A certificate "issued" by a chamber of commerce, an "international registry" or a law firm is not a registration document.
  • Ask for the official versionThe register issues a certificate on the registration or liquidation of a legal entity for free, within 3 days, to Armenian citizens, foreigners and legal entities, online through the national ID login (service page, read 13 Sep 2026). A real counterparty can request one in its own name and send it to you.

Step three

Who is behind it: declarations, extract, and what is not recorded

Declared beneficial owners, free. On the card, "Company BOR declarations" lists each beneficial-ownership declaration with its filing time. Every legal entity must file one within 40 days of registration and within 40 days of a change, and confirm or update it every year by 20 February; the obligation was phased in from September 2021 and covered all legal entities, including LLCs owned only by individuals, from 1 January 2023 (MB Legal; Armenian Lawyer, 18 Feb 2026; both law firms, secondary). The Ucom card above fits the pattern, with a declaration filed every February since 2023. A company with no declaration at all, years after registration, is a question to ask. Our beneficial-owners guide covers how to read one and what "ground 3" means.

The extract, AMD 3,000. Founders and participants, the director, the charter and legal succession are in the "Company information request" service: extract plus articles of association, AMD 3,000, issued immediately, ordered online with the national ID login; the service page lists Armenian citizens as eligible applicants (service page, read 13 Sep 2026). Non-citizens without that login need someone in Armenia to order it or to ask the Agency directly; we could not confirm an in-person fee.

Not recorded at all. The register records participants only for limited liability companies. Its FAQ states that "the Agency does not maintain the registry of joint-stock companies" and does not record members of commercial cooperatives (FAQ). For a CJSC or OJSC, the declarations are the only ownership view the register offers.

Step four

Signs that the company actually trades

Registration costs little and proves little. These public sources, keyed by the same tax ID, show whether a company does business with the state, pays tax at scale, or holds the licence its offer would need.

  • Public contracts (PPCM)Armenia's procurement registry at armeps.am/ppcm/public/contracts lists signed state contracts with the buyer, value, date and procedure, each row carrying the supplier's 8-digit tax ID. A supplier with years of contracts from several ministries is a going concern. No contracts proves nothing; most private firms never sell to the state. Our weekly index held 236,159 contracts as of 29 Aug 2026.
  • Tax service searchThe State Revenue Committee's taxpayer search system takes a tax ID or a name behind an image captcha. It is a manual cross-check that the tax authority knows the number; we have not documented which fields it returns.
  • Largest taxpayers listThe SRC publishes its list of the 1,000 largest taxpayers every quarter (SRC news; the Q1 2026 list totalled 459.27 billion drams per MassisPost). A company claiming to be a national leader should be on it.
  • Central Bank licenceBanks, credit organisations, investment firms, currency dealers and insurers need a Central Bank of Armenia licence; the Central Bank links the list for each type from its financial institutions page, and banks are also listed at cba.am/en/licensed-banks. A registered LLC offering deposits, loans, trading accounts or "guaranteed returns" without a licence is not a financial institution, however real its registration.
  • Central Bank warning listIn January 2026 the Central Bank published a list of 72 unlicensed organisations publicly offering financial services (Finport, 15 Jan 2026); the list itself is in Armenian at cba.am/hy/unlicensed-organizations. Earlier warnings named brands such as "DaoBit", "Orbis" (Orbis Exchange) and "GKGD" (ArmBanks, 24 Nov 2025).

Before you pay

Scam patterns and the check that catches each

  • "Licensed and registered in Armenia"Common in forex, crypto and loan offers. Registration is at the Ministry of Justice; a licence is at the Central Bank. Check both. The first without the second is the pattern the Central Bank's warnings describe.
  • The borrowed identityA real, well-known company's name and tax ID on a website, email or certificate that the company never issued. The register confirms the company, not the sender. Contact the company through a channel you find yourself, not the phone number or email in the offer.
  • The brand with no entityA trading name that appears nowhere in the register. Ask for the legal name and tax ID; a real business sends them in a minute because they are on every invoice it issues.
  • New company, old story"Fifteen years of experience" with a registration last spring and no declarations filed. Not proof of fraud, but a reason to ask for the extract.
  • Pay someone elseAn invoice from an Armenian company asking for payment to a personal card, a crypto wallet or a company in a third country. Money for an Armenian company goes to an account in that company's name.
  • The forged certificateCovered above: any field that differs from the card ends the check.
