The Georgian company extract in English: how to get it, and when it is enough

A bank abroad, a visa officer or a foreign partner asked for "the company registration extract, in English". What they are asking for

One document with a long name

In Georgia the official proof that a company exists, who runs it and who owns it is the extract (ამონაწერი) from the "Registry of Entrepreneurs and Non-Entrepreneurial (Non-Commercial) Legal Entities", kept by the National Agency of Public Registry (NAPR). Asked for a "certificate of incorporation", a "certificate of good standing" or a "company registration extract" from Georgia, this is the document to send: it carries the status, directors, partners and any liquidation or insolvency proceedings in one signed file, and English-language guides treat it as Georgia's equivalent of a certificate of good standing (georgiaregister.net, a secondary source, read 13 Sep 2026).

Anyone can order it for any company. NAPR's own FAQ says the extract "is issued to any person upon a relevant request" (napr.gov.ge FAQ, checked 13 Sep 2026). No power of attorney is needed, which matters when the company is your counterparty rather than your own.

The register itself is kept in Georgian. NAPR's search site says so on its English front page: under Article 14 of the General Administrative Code the language of proceedings is Georgian, "therefore registered data as name, surname and firm name can be found in Georgian" (enreg.reestri.gov.ge, checked 13 Sep 2026). The English extract is NAPR's own rendering of those records.

What you receive

What the English extract looks like

We read an English extract a Georgian non-profit published about itself, prepared by NAPR in December 2023. We do not link it here because, like every extract, it prints the personal numbers of the people on its board. Its structure is the one you should expect:

  • Header"Ministry of Justice of Georgia, LEPL National Agency of Public Registry", the document title in English, and the application serial number with the date and time of preparation.
  • SubjectFirm Name, Legal Form, Identification Number (the 9-digit code), Registration Number and Date, Registering Authority, Legal Address. Names and the address are written in Latin letters.
  • Additional InformationEmail and telephone, with the note that the company, not the registry, answers for their accuracy.
  • ProceedingsInformation on Liquidation, Reorganization or Insolvency Proceedings. "Not registered" is the answer a bank wants to see.
  • PeopleGoverning Body and Management/Representative Powers: directors and who may sign alone or jointly. For companies, the partners and their shares appear here too.
  • EncumbrancesSeizure/Injunction, Tax Lien/Mortgage, Pledge/Leasing on Intangible or Movable Property, Debtor Registry.
  • FooterAuthenticity "can be verified through the official web-page" of NAPR, and the channels where the extract can be obtained: napr.gov.ge, territorial registration offices, Public Service Halls and NAPR's authorised persons.

What it is not: a translation of the company's charter or of the filings behind it. Those stay in Georgian in the registration file. If a receiver wants the charter in English, that is a separate translation job.

Step by step

Order the extract with the English version

  1. Get the 9-digit identification code. Search the company free at enreg.reestri.gov.ge by name (Georgian script matches best) or by code; no account, no captcha for the results list. Our guide to the NAPR search walks through it. Ordering by code avoids paying for a namesake.
  2. Pick a channel. Online: NAPR's "Preparation of extract" link opens the government portal my.gov.ge, which needs a login. In person: any Public Service Hall (იუსტიციის სახლი) or NAPR territorial office, with your passport. Online is cheaper. From abroad, a NAPR authorised person or anyone in Georgia can order it for you, since no relationship with the company is required.
  3. Ask for the English-language version at the same time. NAPR's tariff says the English extract "is prepared together with any service" for an extra 26 GEL. Tick it when you order.
  4. Choose the speed and pay. Next working day or same day; the prices are in the table below.
  5. Receive a signed PDF. The online extract is an electronic document. Keep the file as issued. For an apostille, PSDA accepts the electronic document itself (e-Apostille) or a printout certified by an authorised person; see below.
  6. Check it against the registry. On enreg, "Search Application" accepts an application number, so the serial number printed at the top of the extract can be looked up; the subject search by code should show the same name, legal form and status. Do the same with any extract a counterparty sends you.

We found no official page, on 13 Sep 2026, stating whether a foreign passport alone opens a my.gov.ge account. The counter and an authorised person work regardless.

Before you pay for more

Will the receiver accept it as it is?

There are three levels of formality. Ask the receiver in writing which one they need, how recent the extract must be, and whether a PDF will do. The answer decides whether you spend 39 GEL or several hundred.

  • Level 1: the English extract aloneOften enough for foreign counterparties, supplier onboarding, marketplace and payment-provider checks, and many banks' KYC teams.
  • Level 2: English extract plus apostilleUsually required by foreign courts, notaries, company registries, consulates and immigration offices in countries that are party to the Hague Apostille Convention. It confirms the signature and seal, not the content.
  • Level 3: plus a translation into the receiver's languageAn authority working in another language may insist on its own, often from a translator it recognises.
  • Outside the Apostille ConventionCountries that are not party to it need legalisation instead: a two-step process run through Georgia's Public Service Development Agency and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the receiving country's consulate (sda.gov.ge, checked 13 Sep 2026). Check the HCCH status table first.

Level 2

The apostille: PSDA, 30 to 150 GEL, or 50 GEL electronic

Georgia's competent authority for apostilles is the Public Service Development Agency (PSDA) of the Ministry of Justice (HCCH authority listing, checked 13 Sep 2026). You apply at a PSDA office or any Public Service Hall; "anyone interested may apply". Documents issued by administrative bodies of Georgia qualify, and so does "the printed electronic document certified by an authorized person or the electronic copy", which covers an extract ordered online.

Order of operations: get the extract first, then apostille the registry's document, not a copy you made.

Georgia launched the e-Apostille in late 2020 (Georgia Today, 28 Dec 2020); the old verification address apostille.cra.ge now redirects to PSDA's apostille checker. PSDA also lists "documents issued by the administrative body related to customs and commercial operations" as not apostillable; the Hague Conference reads that exclusion narrowly and company extracts are apostilled routinely, but if a clerk hesitates, that line is why.

Level 3

When you need a translation anyway

Option A: NAPR's English version. The cheapest official English at 26 GEL, with no translator to vouch for. English only.

Option B: PSDA's translation plus apostille. The agency that apostilles also translates documents it certifies, at 30 GEL per page, into English, Italian, French, Spanish, Greek, Russian, German or Turkish, and it can apostille its own translation (sda.gov.ge, checked 13 Sep 2026). It is the one-counter route for German, French or Italian.

Option C: a translator plus a Georgian notary. A notary certifies the translation's accuracy or the translator's signature. Georgia's notary tariff (Government Resolution 507, English text as amended to 2013) sets GEL 1 per page for certifying a translation and GEL 2 for a signature, before VAT and before the translator's own fee, which is agreed freely (notary.ge PDF, read 13 Sep 2026; the text may have been amended since). Watch the catch: PSDA's list of documents it will not apostille includes "notarial acts: certification of the authenticity of a copy of the original document and translation from one language to another". A notarised translation is useful inside Georgia; for abroad, use option B or translate in the destination country.

Option D: a sworn or certified translator in the destination country. Some authorities accept only their own court-listed translators. Send the apostilled extract; they translate both.

Never present a machine translation of the Georgian PDF as the company's extract.

What goes wrong

Four mistakes we see

  • Ordering "information" instead of the extractNAPR's tariff has a service called "Preparation of information from the Registry" at 13 GEL and 4 working days. It is not the extract. Ask for the extract by name.
  • Translating the brand nameThe English extract uses the registered name in Latin letters, which often differs from the trading name on the website. Match the identification code, not the name, when you fill in a foreign form.
  • Submitting a stale extractDirectors, addresses and partners change. An old extract naming the previous director invites a rejection.
  • Expecting the ownership chainThe extract names the direct partners. If a partner is another company, its owners are in that company's own extract: one document per layer.
