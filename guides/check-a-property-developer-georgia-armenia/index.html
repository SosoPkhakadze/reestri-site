Start here

A developer check has two halves

The company. The name on the billboard is a brand. The name on your contract is a legal entity with a registration date, a status, directors and owners, recorded in the business register: NAPR in Georgia, the State Register of Legal Entities in Armenia.

The property. The plot and, later, your apartment are records in the property registry: NAPR again in Georgia, the Cadastre Committee in Armenia. The building permit is a third record, held by the city.

A clean company on a plot it does not own is a problem. So is a clean plot owned by a company registered last month. Do both halves.

Scope

A company check is not a title check. Anything in this guide about the developer company, including the paid report named near the end, tells you about the company. It does not confirm who owns the plot or your future apartment, whether a mortgage or seizure is registered on it, or whether the permit is valid.

For that, use the official property record: the NAPR property extract in Georgia and the Cadastre Committee extract in Armenia, both described below.

Georgia, step one

Check the developer company in NAPR

Georgia's business register is free to search and needs no account. Our Georgian company guide walks through the portal screen by screen; here is the short version for a developer.

  1. Get the legal name and the 9 digit identification code from the contract, not from the brochure. A sales office that will not give you the code before you pay is giving you an answer.
  2. Search the code at enreg.reestri.gov.ge. The results row shows the name, the legal form (most developers are LLCs, შპს) and the status. Active is what you want; a registration suspended under article 254 usually means tax trouble.
  3. Open the detail view (a 3 digit image check stands in front of it) and read the registration date and the list of registry actions. Compare the date with when sales started and when the permit was issued.
  4. Order the extract if the money is serious. Founders, partners and their shares, directors, and sections for seizures, tax liens and the debtor registry are in the official extract. Online it costs 13 GEL for the next working day or 52 GEL the same day; an English version adds 26 GEL (NAPR fees). Our extract guide explains how to order it.
  5. Look at the partners' other companies. The register's search by person or company shows where a founder appears elsewhere. A new LLC for each project is normal; what you want is the group's track record, including earlier project companies that are liquidated or suspended.

Armenia, step one

Check the developer company in e-register.moj.am

The Ministry of Justice register is free, in English and needs no login to search. Our Armenian company guide covers it in depth; for a developer the essentials are these.

  1. Take the 8 digit tax ID from the contract and paste it into e-register.moj.am/en/search/companies. The search accepts a tax ID, a registration number or a name. Most companies are registered in Armenian script, so a Latin name search can miss or hit the wrong entity; the tax ID does not.
  2. Read the card: the status sentence, the registration date, the tax ID and the address. A card with an added restriction line deserves a direct question to the developer.
  3. Read the beneficial owner declarations. The card lists every declaration with its filing date; companies must file within 40 days of registration and confirm each year by 20 February (MB Legal). Our beneficial owners guide shows how to read one. A developer with no declaration years after registration is a question to ask.
  4. Check public contracts under the same tax ID at armeps.am. A construction company with years of state contracts has a verifiable history. No contracts proves nothing; most residential developers never build for the state.
  5. The extract with founders, participants and the charter costs AMD 3,000 online, but the service lists Armenian citizens as applicants (service page, read 13 Sep 2026). A foreign buyer can ask the developer to order it and send it.

Georgia, step two

Who owns the plot, and what is registered on it

Georgian property records are public. The Law on Public Registry defines access to information as "ensuring the freedom of viewing and issuance of information and documents retained at the Public Registry" (Law on Public Registry, Article 2). Everything hangs on the cadastral code.

  1. Ask for the cadastral code of the plot, a dotted number built from zone, sector, block and plot as printed on NAPR extracts. It belongs on the preliminary contract. If the seller will not give it, find the plot yourself on NAPR's map at maps.gov.ge.
  2. Order the property extract through the NAPR service "Preparation of an extract from the real estate register and a cadastral plan" on my.gov.ge, linked from NAPR's real estate registry page. NAPR's extract fee is 13 GEL for the next working day online, 52 GEL the same day, 26 GEL extra in English (NAPR fees). my.gov.ge needs an account; if you cannot open one, the extract is also issued at any Public Service Hall.
  3. Check the owner's section against the contract. The owner should be the developer company, or a landowner whose agreement with the developer you can see. If nobody can show you why the developer may build on the plot, stop.
  4. Read the mortgage and obligation sections. A bank mortgage on a development plot is common financing, but your contract has to say how your apartment is released from it. A seizure or injunction is different: find out why before paying anything.

Georgia, step three

Is the building permitted: Tbilisi and Batumi

Ask the developer for the permit order: its number, its date and the issuing service. Then check it against what is being sold.

  • Tbilisi: the Architecture ServicePermits in Tbilisi come from the city's Architecture Service, whose portal tas.ge has an interactive map and a search for its decisions. Published decisions on docs.tbilisi.gov.ge show the order number and date, the result, the plot's cadastral code and its registered owners with company ID codes: a second check that the permit and the land belong to the company selling to you.
  • Tbilisi: suspended sitesIn November 2025 Tbilisi City Hall launched a public map showing buildings officially approved for operation and construction sites where work has been suspended, both from the Municipal Inspection (Georgia Today, 27 Nov 2025).
  • Batumi: Architecture and Urban Planning ServiceBatumi City Hall's Architecture and Urban Planning Service prepares permits in three separate stages: land use conditions, agreement of the architectural project, and the construction permit itself. It also keeps the permit register and handles legalisation of buildings built without a permit or against the project (service statute on matsne, in Georgian). Approved land use conditions are not a construction permit; ask which stage the project has actually reached.
  • Floors and areasCompare the permitted project with the sales plan. If you are offered an apartment on a floor the permit does not cover, that is the first question to raise.

Armenia, steps two and three

The cadastre and the permit in Armenia

Plot and restrictions. The Cadastre Committee issues a property clearance extract on a real estate unit, other information on the rights and restrictions registered to it, and information on the availability of property belonging to the same entity. Applications can be submitted online at e-cadastre.am after registering, and the information service is paid (Cadastre Committee, information provision). That last service answers a useful question directly: does the developer company own the plot it is building on?

The permit in Yerevan. The municipality publishes a construction permissions map at yerevan.am/en/permits, marked beta. It filters by district, purpose, address and developer (Կառուցապատող), covers both construction permits and architectural planning assignments, and shows the permit number with its start and end dates. Search the developer's name in Armenian script as well as in Latin letters.

Outside Yerevan. Municipalities issue construction permits; since 1 January 2025 applications go only through the government platform urban.e-gov.am (Poghosyan Legal Consulting via Legal 500, 1 Dec 2025). The platform needs a national ID login, so ask the developer for the permit document and check it with the municipality.

The tax refund promise. If a sales office sells on Armenia's income tax refund for mortgage interest, check the permit date: authorities halted the scheme in Yerevan in 2025, but developers who received construction permits before 2022 can still sell apartments under it (JAMnews, 9 Apr 2026).

What the law protects

Where your payments are protected, and where they are not

Georgia. A Tbilisi law firm wrote in April 2025 that "Georgia lacks specific legislation regulating shared participation in construction", so off plan sales rest on the general Civil Code (Just Advisors, 15 Apr 2025). We did not find a developer or escrow law adopted in 2025 or 2026. If a salesperson says your payments are held in escrow by law, ask for the law's name and the account agreement. Otherwise your protection is the contract, the registry record and your own checks.

Armenia. The Civil Code has rules for developers' special accounts. According to a law firm summary, amendments in force since 15 October 2023 limit what a developer may take from the special account before the buyer registers ownership: from 1 January 2025, up to 50 percent, then more in proportion to completion up to 95 percent, and the last 5 percent within a year after registration if there are no defects (360 Business Law). A Ministry of Economy draft that would route advance payments only into the special account or an irrevocable letter of credit is listed as under discussion (e-draft.am project 5682, read 14 Sep 2026). We could not read the consolidated official Code, so ask the developer in writing which bank holds your project's special account, and have the contract name it.

Before you pay

Red flags and the check that catches each

These are patterns worth a question, not proof of fraud. Each one comes with the record that shows it.

  • Registered weeks before salesA company registered shortly before the sales launch, selling a large project, with no earlier projects in the group. Registration date: register card. Group history: the partners in the extract and their other companies.
  • The land is not the developer'sThe owner in the property extract is another person or company and there is no agreement you can see. Check: Georgian property extract owner section, Tbilisi permit decision owner line, Armenian cadastre information.
  • Contract company differs from the brandOften a project company, which is normal. Ask how it links to the brand and check that its partners are the group you think you are buying from.
  • Earlier project companies failedA founder's other companies include liquidated or suspended ones, or ones with seizures. Check: person and company search in the Georgian register; declarations and cards in Armenia.
  • Seizure on the plot or the companyA court or authority is already involved. Find out why before signing.
  • Permit stage or floors do not matchOnly land use conditions approved, or more floors sold than permitted, or a Tbilisi site on the suspended map. Check the permit order and the city map.
  • Pay someone elseA request to pay a director personally, a third company or a card. Money for an apartment goes to the account of the company named on the contract.
