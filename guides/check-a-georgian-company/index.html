How to check a Georgian company in the NAPR register

Status, ID code, directors and owners: which of them are free, which sit behind a captcha, which cost 13 GEL and four working days. Written for anyone who has a Georgian company name on an invoice, a contract, a job offer or a deposit request and wants the public record before the money moves.

Step one

Get the 9-digit identification code

Every legal entity in Georgia has a 9-digit identification code (საიდენტიფიკაციო კოდი). It is the registry number and the tax number in one; there is no separate VAT number. It appears on invoices, receipts, contracts, the footer of most company websites and the About page of most Facebook shops. Individual entrepreneurs (ი/მ) are registered under the person's own name and 11-digit personal number instead.

Ask for the code before you ask for anything else. A real company gives it instantly; it is printed on everything they issue. Names are ambiguous (the register holds many namesakes and branches, and the registered name is in Georgian script), but the code resolves to exactly one record. If a counterparty will not give you its identification code, that is already your answer.

Step two

The free search at enreg.reestri.gov.ge

The National Agency of Public Registry (NAPR, საჯარო რეესტრი) runs the register. Its public search needs no account and no captcha. The English toggle translates the labels only; the data stays Georgian.

  1. Open enreg.reestri.gov.ge/main.php and choose the subject search (სუბიექტის ძებნა, "Search subject"). Switch the interface to English if you prefer; it changes the form labels, nothing else.
  2. Type the identification code in the ID field, or a name (Georgian script matches best; a Latin fragment sometimes works) in the name field. Leave legal form on "all". Press search.
  3. Read the results table. Five rows per page; columns are identification code, personal number (individual entrepreneurs only), name, legal form and status. This much is enough to say whether the company exists and whether it is active.
  4. Click the info icon on a row to open the detail view. The site asks you to type a 3-digit number from an image. This is the captcha that stops automated reading; a person types it in two seconds.
  5. In the detail view read the registration date, the state registration number, the list of registry actions (applications with dates and service types) and the scanned or prepared documents attached to each action. Past extracts attached to old applications are downloadable here without paying.

"Registered" is not the same as trading. A company can sit in the register as active for a decade without an employee or a bank account. Status tells you it has not been suspended or dissolved; the registration date and the list of registry actions tell you whether anything has happened since.

The honest map

What is public in Georgia and what is not

  • Free, no captchaName, identification code, legal form, status. Plus the reverse search: every company a named person or organisation participates in, with the role. This is the layer any script, including ours, can read.
  • Free, after a captchaRegistration date, state registration number, the full list of registry actions with dates, and the documents attached to them: scanned filings, decisions, earlier extracts. NAPR treats the entire registration file as public. That includes founders' passport scans in older filings; if you are a founder, assume everything you filed is visible.
  • Paid, officialThe current extract (ამონაწერი): directors and representatives, partners with their share sizes, capital, liens and pledges, address, email, phone. Anyone can order it for any company. See the next section.
  • Not public at allBank accounts, revenue, tax filings, employee counts, court cases (those live at info.court.ge, by party name). Ownership chains above the first level are not in the register; you climb them one extract at a time.
  • No APINAPR publishes no API, no bulk file and no open-data release of the business register. Mirrors like companyinfo.ge (Transparency International Georgia) are scrapes that went stale when the captcha arrived; OpenSanctions' Georgian snapshot is dated 13 Aug 2024. Use them for history, never as the current record.

Owners and directors

Order the extract: 13 to 52 GEL, any company, any person

Georgia's Law on Public Registry says an extract "shall be given out to any person". You need no relationship with the company and no power of attorney. The extract is a signed PDF, dated on the day it is prepared, and it lists the persons authorised to manage and represent the company, the partners with their shares, the capital, and any lien, pledge or injunction on the record. It is the document banks, notaries and courts accept.

Online: register at my.gov.ge (a Georgian ID or a foreign passport plus a phone number), find "Preparation of extract from the Registry of Entrepreneurs", enter the identification code, pay by card. In person: any branch of the House of Justice (იუსტიციის სახლი, the Public Service Hall) prepares the same document at the counter; bring a passport.

An English-language version is prepared together with any order for an extra 26 GEL. Older extracts attached to past applications can be downloaded free from the detail view described above; they show the record as it was on their date, which is sometimes exactly what you want.

The reverse search

From a person or a holding company to every company they are in

The same portal has a participant search (მონაწილის ძებნა) that almost nobody outside Georgia knows about. Type a first name and last name, or an organisation's name, and it returns every company where that party is a partner, director, founder or representative, with the company's identification code and the participation type. No captcha, no account.

It answers questions the forward search cannot: does the director who signed your contract run six other companies, three of them suspended? Is the "group" a real holding with registered subsidiaries or one LLC and a logo? Which Georgian entities does a foreign parent own directly?

Two cautions. Georgian names are common and the search matches on name text, so a result set for "Giorgi Beridze" mixes several people; the personal-number column separates them only for individual entrepreneurs. And the register is a business record: use it to check companies and the people who run them, never to locate or profile a private person.

Before you pay

The scam checklist

Each line is a question the register answers in under a minute. The pattern behind most complaints we read (rental deposits, surrogacy agencies, apartment developers, call-centre jobs, residency-for-a-fee offers) is a real-looking brand with no matching legal entity behind it.

  • Deposit first?Never before the registry check. A legitimate Georgian business will wait ten minutes while you look it up; a scam will not.
  • Invoice name vs registry nameThe legal name on the invoice or contract, and the identification code next to it, must match a record in the register letter for letter. "Global Surrogacy" as a brand is meaningless; შპს plus a registered name plus a code is checkable.
  • StatusActive. Suspended under article 254 means the company is not in good standing with the tax authority; liquidated means it does not exist.
  • Registration date vs claimed history"Fifteen years in business" with a registration date last spring is a lie or a new shell; either way, ask.
  • Director matchThe extract names the person authorised to sign. The person signing your contract or receiving your transfer should be that person or hold a written power of attorney from them.
  • Bank account holderMoney should go to an account in the company's name at a Georgian bank, never to a personal card or a foreign account "for convenience".
  • Tax standingThe Revenue Service's taxpayer registry at rs.ge shows VAT registration and tax liens by identification code. It is behind a reCAPTCHA, so it is a manual check; do it for larger deals.
  • Job offers and residency schemesAn employer with no legal name in the advert, or a company registered three months ago hiring fifty "sales operators" on Telegram, is answering its own question. Paying to be "signed onto" someone's company for a residence permit is fraud on both sides and grounds for refusal.

Alphabet

Georgian names, Latin names, and why the code wins

A Georgian company's registered name is in Mkhedruli, the Georgian script. Its trade name in Latin letters is a marketing choice and rarely a letter-for-letter transliteration: Silknet is registered as შპს სილქნეტი, which the national transliteration renders as "shps silkneti". Searching "Silknet" in the name field can surface branches, holdings and namesakes before the operating company; searching the code 204952685 returns one row.

Practical habits: copy the Georgian name from the company's own website footer or its Facebook About page and paste it; search a short Georgian fragment rather than the full name (the search is a substring match); and whenever a document carries a 9-digit code, search the code and ignore the name entirely. The tool below returns both the Georgian original and a transliteration on every record for the same reason: the two rarely agree and you will need both.
