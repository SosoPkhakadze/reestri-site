From the data · story 1

Armenia cut single-source contracts from 52 percent of spend to 7. The habit stayed.

We indexed 236,159 signed contracts from PPCM, the public contracts module of Armenia's procurement portal armeps.am, and classified every award by its legal procedure form. By value, non-competitive awards collapsed. By count, roughly four procedures in ten are still awarded without competition.

By Soso Pkhakadze · published 2 Sep 2026 · registry index of 29 Aug 2026 · aggregate only, companies only

The headline

The money moved to tenders. The count did not follow.

Between 2018 and August 2026 the registry records 214,244 contracts worth 4,547,341,742,554 AMD. Of that, 70,606 contracts (32.96 percent) worth 980,736,051,708 AMD (21.57 percent) were awarded under PPCM's two single-source procedure forms, ՄԱ and ՀՄԱ. The value share fell from 52.04 percent in 2018 to 6.83 percent in 2024, then ticked back up to 12.54 percent in 2026 to date.

One buyer signs almost half of all single-source money. The Ministry of Health awarded 486,685,298,874 AMD without competition across 6,788 contracts: 70.04 percent of everything it spent through PPCM and 47.12 percent of all single-source value in the registry. The top 5 buyers hold 72.11 percent of it, the top 30 hold 95.02 percent.

The biggest single-source suppliers are the state's own companies and hospitals. Public Television of Armenia, Electric Networks of Armenia, HayPost and Gazprom Armenia lead the list; 11 of the top 30 are hospitals or medical institutions paid by the Ministry of Health. The top 30 together account for 44.74 percent of all single-source value.

1,080 companies live on one state buyer. Among companies with at least 10 contracts, 1,080 get more than 90 percent of their public revenue from a single buyer, 1,675,307,582,513 AMD in total; 618 of them have exactly one buyer ever. 430 depend on the Ministry of Health, 19 road and construction contractors on the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Infrastructure. Dependence is not the same as favoritism: 268 of the 1,080 won all of it competitively, while 224 got at least 90 percent of it single-source.

Appeals are almost extinct in the record. 651 of 214,244 contracts (0.30 percent) carry an appeal narrative: 142 in 2018, 24 in 2023, 18 in the first eight months of 2026.

Method and caveats

What was counted, and what the data cannot say

  • What counts as single-sourcePPCM's own procedure labels ՄԱ (single source) and ՀՄԱ (urgent single source). This is a legal procedure type under Armenia's Law on Procurement, not a judgement about whether competition was possible. Negotiated procedures without notice (ԲԸԱՀ, a label that disappears after 2017) are reported separately. The full procedure vocabulary with counts is in the repo.
  • Unit and valueOne PPCM contract record; year of signature; stated total value in AMD. Six records carry keyed-in totals up to 1.66 quadrillion AMD while their current value is under 1.5 billion; records above 50 billion AMD are dropped from value sums and kept in counts; summary.json lists all six.
  • The health-ministry caveatMinistry of Health figures include payments to public hospitals under state-order medical services, which PPCM records as single-source contracts. That is the single largest driver of the 2018 to 2020 value shares.
  • What PPCM does not publishThe legal ground for each single-source award, unit prices, amendment history, payments, subcontractors, supplier ownership. A falling share can reflect both policy and how buyers route spending through PPCM.
  • Dates and coverage2018 to 2026; coverage before 2018 is thin. 62 contracts with impossible years and 3 without a date are excluded. 2026 covers signatures to 29 Aug. Nothing here was reviewed by a lawyer or by PPCM; rerun the script against the next weekly index and the figures update.
  • PrivacyAggregate only. Supplier names and tax IDs appear only for legal entities; individual entrepreneurs are counted in totals but never listed. The index strips emails, bank details and staff names at ingest.
