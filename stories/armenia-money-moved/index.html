From the data · story 2

The single-source money left the Ministry of Health. Here is where it went.

In 2018 the Ministry of Health awarded 62.9 billion AMD without competition, 95.70 percent of its spend. In 2026 so far: 0.8 billion, 5.98 percent. A second pass over the 236,159 contracts in PPCM, Armenia's procurement registry, traces the non-competitive money to its new addresses and watches the supplier base grow 2.5 times.

By Soso Pkhakadze · published 2 Sep 2026 · registry index of 29 Aug 2026 · aggregate only, companies only

The headline

Same procedure, new buyers

The single-source money changed hands. The largest single-source buyer of 2024 to 2026 is now the Administration of the Council of Public Broadcaster with 22,908,930,783 AMD, then Yerevan Municipality (16,595,935,837 AMD), the Ministry of High-Tech Industry (13,102,736,176 AMD; its share jumped from 2.93 percent in 2024 to 68.16 percent in 2026), the Ministry of Health (12,468,608,603 AMD) and the Probation Service (11,741,474,959 AMD). The top 25 buyers hold 90.09 percent of the 192.3 billion AMD awarded single-source in 2024 to 2026; the top 5 hold 39.95 percent.

34 of 185 active buyers still ran mostly on single-source in 2026, measured as more than half their yearly spend: 42 buyers in 2018, 24 in 2024, 34 in 2026. Buyers that jumped in 2026: Ministry of Environment (13.97 percent in 2024 to 65.32), State Property Management Committee (0.36 to 76.54), Armaeronavigatsia CJSC (6.15 to 65.50), Ministry of High-Tech Industry (2.93 to 68.16).

The supplier base grew 2.54 times and its top thinned. 3,072 suppliers won at least one contract in 2018; 7,795 in 2024. The top 10 suppliers held 30.12 percent of contract value in 2018 and 17.24 percent in 2026. Every year since 2022, more than a third of winning suppliers are new to the registry; the 15 largest companies that entered since 2022 took 358.0 billion AMD, and 12 of the 15 have a single-source share of exactly zero.

The registry is splitting into many tiny contracts and a few huge ones. The median contract fell from 569,600 AMD in 2018 to 330,000 AMD in 2025. Contracts above 1 billion AMD went from 21 in 2018 (17.78 percent of value) to 243 in 2024 (59.48 percent of value).

Method and caveats

What was counted, and what the data cannot say

  • Same base as story 1Unit, year, value, supplier and buyer keys, the 50 billion AMD outlier cap (6 keyed-in records dropped from value sums, kept in counts) and the ՄԱ / ՀՄԱ single-source mapping are identical to story 1. Both windows reconcile exactly: 214,244 contracts, 4,547,341,742,554 AMD, of which 21.57 percent single-source.
  • "New" means new to PPCMA supplier's first contract is its earliest signature date in the registry. Coverage before 2018 is thin, so a supplier first seen in 2022 may have sold to the state before PPCM, and a company that re-registered under a new tax ID also looks new. The 2018 to 2021 cohorts are treated as context only.
  • Signed totals, not paymentsConcentration is measured on signed contract values. A framework agreement signed in one year and drawn down over several inflates that year. The 2024 spike in billion-AMD contracts coincides with large road and construction contracts by the Territorial Administration Ministry and the Armenian Territorial Development Fund.
  • Procedure form, not legal groundPPCM records the award procedure, not the reason for it. A high single-source share can be a state-order arrangement (hospitals, the public broadcaster) rather than a discretionary choice. Buyer names come from PPCM as typed; rows are keyed by tax ID, so totals hold even where the displayed name varies.
  • 2026 is year to dateSignatures to 29 Aug 2026; the 2026 shares will move. Nothing here was reviewed by a lawyer or by PPCM; rerun the script against the next weekly index and the figures update.
  • PrivacyAggregate only. Supplier names and tax IDs appear only for legal entities; individual entrepreneurs are counted in totals and cohort sizes but never listed.
